Bring the Right Facts Into Focus

What counsel may ask about.

This is not a request to submit a detailed account online. After conflicts are cleared, counsel may ask about:

The exact charge and code section
The stop, encounter or accident
Field observations and testing
Breath or blood procedures
Statements and recordings
The license or administrative status
Prior driving or related history
Court, arrest and release paperwork
Employment, CDL, licensing or security concerns
More Than One Timeline May Matter

Court, license and daily-life decisions can overlap.

The criminal case and driving consequences may not follow a single calendar. The first task is to identify every date on the paperwork and determine which actions, if any, are required. Do not rely on a general website to calculate a specific deadline.

Questions Clients Often Ask

Will I lose my license?

Possible driving consequences depend on the charge, test or refusal issues, prior history, court orders and current law. Counsel must review the specific record.

Do I have to appear in court?

Appearance requirements depend on the charge, court and case status. Do not miss court based on general information. Ask counsel about the specific summons.

Should I send my full story through the website?

No. Start with the charge, parties, court and date. Wait until the firm confirms it can speak with you before sending confidential detail.

Can a result be guaranteed?

No. Outcomes depend on evidence, law, court and case-specific facts.

Why Early Organization Helps

Information gets harder to find over time.

Documents, video, receipts, location information and witnesses can become harder to identify over time. Counsel can explain what should be preserved and what should not be created or altered. Do not contact witnesses, post about the matter or destroy information based on general advice.
