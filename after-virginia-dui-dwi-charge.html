After a Virginia DUI or DWI charge: what to organize first

The period after a charge can feel like one urgent problem. There may be connected questions involving court, driving status, evidence, work and family obligations. Organization helps separate what requires action from what requires advice.

Prepared August 2026Attorney review required before publication

Keep the paperwork together

Preserve the summons, warrant, release documents, license paperwork, property receipt and other papers received. Do not write on originals. Calendar every date, then ask counsel which dates require action and what appearance is expected.

Record sources, not a polished story

After speaking with counsel, it may be useful to identify receipts, location data, photographs, video sources, communications or witnesses. Do not contact witnesses, edit records or post about the matter.

Identify work and license consequences early

Tell counsel about a CDL, driving-dependent job, professional license, security clearance or out-of-state license. These concerns may affect priorities even when they do not change the charge itself.

Expect more than one legal question

Virginia DUI law includes criminal and driving-related provisions. Testing, refusal, prior history and administrative issues may matter. The correct analysis requires the exact charge, paperwork, facts and current law.

Do not miss court based on general information

Only the court or retained counsel can confirm appearance instructions for a specific case. Contacting a law firm does not automatically continue a date or excuse an appearance.

This article provides general information only and does not create an attorney-client relationship. Start with the charge, court, court date and license state. Do not send a detailed account until conflicts are cleared.
