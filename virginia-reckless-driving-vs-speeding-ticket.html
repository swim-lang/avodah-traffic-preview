Reckless driving or speeding ticket? Why the distinction matters in Virginia

Two drivers may both say they received a speeding ticket while their paperwork describes different kinds of cases. The first task is to read the summons, not rely on the driver's shorthand description.

Prepared August 2026Attorney review required before publication

Check the wording and code section

Virginia law includes reckless-driving provisions for speed and for other alleged conduct. Current law generally describes reckless driving by speed as driving 20 miles per hour or more above the applicable maximum speed limit, or over 85 miles per hour regardless of the applicable limit. The final wording must be checked against current law before publication.

Why the category matters

Virginia generally classifies reckless driving as a Class 1 misdemeanor, while ordinary speeding is usually handled as a traffic infraction. That distinction may affect the court process, possible penalties, criminal-record concerns and driving consequences.

The number is not the entire case

Counsel may examine the alleged speed, posted limit, location, measurement method, paperwork, statements, driving record and local court. No single fact guarantees a reduction, dismissal or other outcome.

Out-of-state drivers should ask two sets of questions

The Virginia case proceeds in Virginia, but the home state may separately treat the result. Appearance and travel questions depend on the court and charge. Do not assume an attorney can appear without the driver unless counsel confirms it.

What to send first

Begin with the exact charge, code section, alleged speed and limit, court, court date and license state. Wait until conflicts are cleared before sending a detailed narrative or documents.
